Technology of Connection // [3000 BCE - 2026 CE]

The Technological Shift

ARCHIVE_83

Moving from physical, localized speech to instantaneous, global networks.

Node_01 // Writing & Surfaces

The First Data Storage

Cuneiform & Clay (3200 BCE): Sumerians utilized reed styluses on wet clay, creating the first durable record-keeping technology.

Papyrus (3000 BCE) & Paper (105 CE): From Egyptian scrolls to Cai Lun’s rag-and-hemp paper in China, information became portable and low-cost.

Node_02 // Mechanical Era

The Printing Revolution

Gutenberg (1440): Combined oil-based ink and metal movable type to break the monopoly of handwritten manuscripts.

Steam Press (1814): Friedrich Koenig applied steam power, accelerating output from hundreds to thousands of pages per hour.

Node_03 // Electrical Pulse

Distance Collapsed

Morse Telegraph (1837): Pulses over copper wires decoupled communication from physical transportation speed.

Telephone (1876) & Radio (1895): Bell converted voice to signals; Marconi transmitted via electromagnetic waves through the air.

Node_04 // Electronic Media

Mass Synchronization

Radio (1920) & TV (1927): Vacuum tubes and Cathode-ray tubes brought synchronized live audio and video to millions.

Transistors (1947): Bell Labs replaced bulky tubes with transistors, enabling the portability of communication devices.

Node_05 // The Digital Network

Packet Switching Era

ARPANET (1969): Introduced packet switching, breaking data into small chunks for efficient routing.

Fiber Optics (1970s): Replaced copper with glass strands, using light pulses to exponentially increase bandwidth.

WWW (1989): Tim Berners-Lee created HTML/HTTP, turning the network into a global ecosystem.

Smartphones (2000s): Microprocessors combined GPS, radio, and computing into a ubiquitous handheld device.
